How to Choose Shaft Flex

By Mike Johnson • April 12, 2024 • 12 min read

Choosing the right shaft flex is one of the most important decisions you'll make for your golf game. The shaft is the engine of your club, and selecting the wrong flex can cost you significant distance and accuracy.

In this comprehensive guide, we'll walk you through everything you need to know about shaft flex, from understanding the different types to matching yours to your swing speed.

What is Shaft Flex?

Shaft flex refers to how much a shaft bends when pressure is applied. A more flexible shaft will bend more during the swing, while a stiffer shaft provides more resistance.

The key concept to understand is that the shaft stores energy as it bends during your swing. At impact, this energy releases, helping to propel the ball forward. If your shaft is too stiff for your swing speed, you won't be able to load it properly, resulting in lost distance.

Understanding Flex Ratings

Shafts typically come in several flex ratings, from most flexible to least:

  • L (Ladies): Designed for swing speeds below 75 mph
  • A (Senior): Designed for swing speeds 75-85 mph
  • R (Regular): Designed for swing speeds 85-95 mph
  • S (Stiff): Designed for swing speeds 95-105 mph
  • X (Extra Stiff): Designed for swing speeds 105-115 mph
  • XX (Extra Extra Stiff): Designed for swing speeds above 115 mph

How to Determine Your Swing Speed

The most accurate way to determine your swing speed is to visit a golf store or driving range with a launch monitor. However, you can get a rough estimate using these guidelines:

Method 1: Estimate from Carry Distance

If you know your average driver carry distance, you can estimate your swing speed:

  • 200 yards or less: 75-85 mph (Senior Flex)
  • 200-230 yards: 85-95 mph (Regular Flex)
  • 230-250 yards: 95-105 mph (Stiff Flex)
  • 250+ yards: 105+ mph (X-Stiff Flex)

Method 2: Use a Radar Gun

If you have access to a radar gun (commonly available at driving ranges), you can get an accurate measurement of your clubhead speed.

Matching Flex to Your Swing

Once you know your swing speed, use this chart to select your flex:

Swing Speed Driver Distance Recommended Flex
Below 75 mph Under 200 yards L (Ladies)
75-85 mph 200-215 yards A (Senior)
85-95 mph 215-230 yards R (Regular)
95-105 mph 230-250 yards S (Stiff)
105-115 mph 250-265 yards X (Extra Stiff)
Above 115 mph 265+ yards XX (Double X)

Common Flex Mistakes to Avoid

Here are the most common mistakes golfers make when choosing shaft flex:

Mistake #1: Choosing Too Stiff

This is the most common error. Golfers often think that stiffer shafts mean more distance. However, if your swing speed can't properly load a stiff shaft, you'll actually lose distance.

Mistake #2: Not Testing Different Flexes

Always test multiple flex options before making a decision. What you're "supposed" to play based on swing speed may not be what actually works best for your swing.

Mistake #3: Ignoring Swing Tempo

Your swing tempo matters. A smooth, rhythmic swinger may benefit from a slightly softer flex, while an aggressive swinger may need something stiffer.

Signs You're Using the Wrong Flex

Watch for these warning signs:

  • Too Stiff: Ball doesn't carry as far as expected, inconsistent contact, feels "boardy"
  • Too Flexible: Ball goes too high, too much spin, lack of control, "whippy" feel

Quick Summary

Choosing the right shaft flex is essential for maximizing your distance and accuracy. Key takeaways:

  • Know your swing speed before buying shafts
  • When in doubt, err toward more flexible rather than less
  • Test before you buy - launch monitors are available at most golf stores
  • Consider your swing tempo, not just swing speed
  • Regular flex works for most recreational golfers
